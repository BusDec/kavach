The operating model has already converged

Three business cases, one attack surface.

SAP runs the money, the people and the maintenance record. The plant runs on PLCs, historians and remote-access paths that were never designed for the internet. The same identities, vendors and networks now connect both. The control gap is where the loss happens.

01 · The system of record

Protect the ERP

SAP holds payables, payroll, procurement, customers and the maintenance history that keeps assets running. A compromised SAP account can change a bank mandate, create a ghost vendor, release a payment run or close a period with bad data.

Business case: prevent fraud and unplanned ERP downtime; shorten the patch window; give the auditor evidence instead of a screenshot.

02 · The operating asset

Protect the plant

OT availability is a physical outcome. A ransomware event or a malicious remote session can stop treatment, generation, dispatch or production. Recovery is not a restore button; it is a safety, environmental and contractual event.

Business case: reduce the probability and duration of an operational outage; know which assets are exposed; rehearse recovery before the incident.

03 · The licence to operate

Protect the data

Employee records, customer data, contractor identity, payroll and health information sit across SAP, HR, cloud and suppliers. India, Europe and the US now expect notification, evidence and accountability on a clock.

Business case: avoid avoidable fines and notification costs; preserve customer trust; answer the board, regulator and insurer with one evidence pack.

Privacy obligations

One estate. Several regulators.

SAP and OT data do not respect jurisdiction. An Indian payroll record, a European employee, a US customer and a contractor on a plant network can all be in the same incident. The notification clocks start at different times, with different tests. Privacy is an architecture and evidence problem before it is a legal one.

JurisdictionMain frameworkBreach clockMaximum penaltyWhat it means for SAP / OT data
European Union GDPR; NIS2 for essential and important entities; DORA for financial entities 72 hours to the supervisory authority after awareness; NIS2 early warning in 24 hours and incident notification in 72 hours Up to €20m or 4% of global annual turnover; NIS2 minimum €10m or 2% for essential entities Employee and customer data in SAP, cloud and suppliers; OT incidents may also be NIS2 incidents. International transfers and processor clauses matter.
United States No federal omnibus privacy law; state laws (e.g. CCPA/CPRA), sector rules (HIPAA, GLBA) and SEC disclosure SEC: material incidents on Form 8-K within four business days of the materiality determination; state breach laws vary CCPA/CPRA: $2,500 per violation, $7,500 per intentional violation or involving minors; HIPAA up to $1.5m per year per category Build a 50-state notification matrix. SEC materiality is a board judgement. Vendor contracts and data maps are evidence.
India Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and DPDP Rules, 2025; CERT-In directions; sector regulators (RBI, SEBI, telecom) CERT-In: report incidents within 6 hours; DPDP breach notification to the Data Protection Board and affected persons without delay, as rules phase in Up to ₹250 crore for failure to take reasonable security safeguards under the DPDP Act Consent, purpose limitation and security safeguards apply to digital personal data in SAP HR, payroll, customer and vendor records. The regime is phasing to full effect in 2027.
United Kingdom UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018 72 hours to the ICO after awareness Up to £17.5m or 4% of global annual turnover Separate from the EU regime; adequacy and transfer analysis still required. Group incidents need a UK-specific assessment.
Singapore Personal Data Protection Act 2012 (PDPA) Notify the PDPC within 3 calendar days of assessing a notifiable breach Up to S$1m or 10% of annual turnover in Singapore Regional hub data, vendor processing and cross-border transfers. A DPO and a tested response plan are expected.

Not legal advice. Rules and thresholds change; verify with counsel for your facts. Last reviewed 18 November 2026.
